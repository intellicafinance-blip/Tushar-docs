Understanding CC & OD

What is Working Capital Finance?

Working capital finance through Cash Credit (CC) and Overdraft (OD) facilities provides businesses with a revolving credit line to manage day-to-day operational expenses without the burden of fixed EMIs.

Cash Credit (CC)

Cash Credit is a running account facility designed for daily business operations. It is primarily secured against your stock (inventory) and debtors (receivables). The bank assigns a drawing power based on the value of your pledged stock and outstanding receivables, which is reviewed periodically.

CC is ideal for businesses that need continuous working capital to purchase raw materials, maintain inventory, and manage production cycles. The drawing power fluctuates based on your stock and debtor statements submitted monthly.

Overdraft (OD)

An Overdraft facility allows you to withdraw funds beyond your current account balance up to a sanctioned limit. OD is typically secured against property, fixed deposits, or other tangible securities. Unlike CC, the OD limit remains fixed throughout the tenure and is not linked to stock levels.

OD is well-suited for businesses that need a safety net for cash flow gaps, seasonal requirements, or those who do not maintain significant stock but have strong assets to offer as security.

Key Benefits of CC/OD

  • Pay interest only on the amount used - Unlike term loans, you are charged interest only on the drawn amount, not the entire sanctioned limit
  • Revolving credit facility - Repay and redraw as many times as needed during the year without reapplying
  • Flexible drawing power - Your available limit adjusts based on your business activity and collateral value
  • No fixed EMI burden - Only monthly interest is debited, preserving your cash flow for business operations
  • Helps maintain FOIR ratio - Since there is no EMI, CC/OD does not impact your Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio, allowing room for other loans
  • Can be used alongside term loans - Businesses often avail both term loans for capex and CC/OD for working capital needs simultaneously

Eligibility

Who Can Apply for CC/OD?

Check if your business qualifies for a Cash Credit or Overdraft facility.

Business Vintage

Minimum 2 years of business operations with a proven track record of revenue and profitability.

GST Registered

Active GST registration with regular filing of GST returns. Turnover reflected in GSTR should match bank statements.

Regular Banking

Consistent banking transactions demonstrating healthy business activity. Good account conduct with no irregularities.

CIBIL Score 675+

A credit score of 675 or above for all partners, directors, or proprietors. Higher scores unlock better rates and higher limits.

Adequate Stock / Debtors (for CC)

For Cash Credit, maintain sufficient inventory and receivables. Stock should be properly stored and insured against relevant risks.

Collateral Property (for OD)

For Overdraft, a clear-title residential or commercial property is required as security. FD-backed OD is also available.

Documentation

Documents Required for CC/OD

Keep these documents ready for a smooth and fast approval process.

Stock & Debtor Documents (for CC)

  • Monthly stock statement in the bank's prescribed format
  • Debtors aging schedule (0-90 days, 90-180 days, 180+ days)
  • Creditors aging schedule
  • Stock insurance policy covering fire, theft, and natural disasters
  • Godown lease agreement (if applicable)

Financial Documents

  • GST returns (GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B) for the last 12-24 months
  • Bank statements for the last 12 months (all operative accounts)
  • Income Tax Returns (ITR) with computation for 2-3 years
  • Audited balance sheet and profit & loss statement for 2-3 years
  • Projected balance sheet and cash flow statement

KYC & Business Documents

  • PAN card and Aadhaar card of proprietor / all partners / directors
  • Business registration certificate / Partnership deed / MOA & AOA
  • GST registration certificate
  • Udyam registration certificate (if MSME)
  • Address proof of business premises

Property Documents (for OD)

  • Complete chain of property title documents
  • Latest property tax receipt
  • Approved building plan and completion certificate
  • Encumbrance certificate (last 13-30 years)
  • Property valuation report from bank-approved valuer

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers to common questions about Cash Credit and Overdraft facilities.

Cash Credit (CC) is a working capital facility where the credit limit is linked to the value of your stock and debtors. You submit monthly stock statements, and the bank adjusts your drawing power accordingly. Overdraft (OD), on the other hand, provides a fixed credit limit secured against property, fixed deposits, or other assets. The key difference is that CC limits fluctuate based on your business activity, while OD limits remain fixed. CC is better suited for trading and manufacturing businesses, while OD works well for service-based businesses or those with strong property assets.
Drawing power is the maximum amount you can withdraw from your CC account at any point. It is calculated by the bank based on the value of your pledged stock and eligible debtors. Typically, banks allow 60-75% of the stock value and 40-50% of debtors aged less than 90 days. You must submit a monthly stock statement to the bank, based on which the drawing power is recalculated. If your stock value decreases, your drawing power also reduces, and you may need to deposit excess funds to stay within the revised limit.
CC and OD facilities are typically sanctioned for one year and require annual renewal. During renewal, the bank reviews your updated financial statements (latest ITR, balance sheet, profit & loss account), bank statement conduct, GST return filings, and a stock audit report (for CC). If your business performance is satisfactory and the account has been conducted well (no irregularities, adequate credit turnover), the bank renews the facility, often with an enhanced limit. The renewal process usually takes 2-4 weeks. It is advisable to initiate the renewal process at least one month before the expiry date.
Interest on CC/OD accounts is calculated on a daily product basis. This means the bank multiplies your outstanding balance each day by the applicable interest rate and divides by 365 (or 366 in a leap year). At the end of each month (or quarter, depending on the bank), the accumulated interest is debited to your account. For example, if you have a CC limit of Rs. 50 Lakh but only use Rs. 20 Lakh for 15 days, you pay interest only on Rs. 20 Lakh for those 15 days. This makes CC/OD significantly more cost-effective than term loans for businesses with fluctuating fund requirements.
Yes, it is very common for businesses to have both a term loan and a CC/OD facility simultaneously. In fact, banks often encourage this combination. The term loan is used for capital expenditure (machinery, equipment, property purchase), while CC/OD handles your day-to-day working capital needs. Since CC/OD does not carry a fixed EMI, it does not significantly impact your FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio), leaving room for term loan eligibility. Many banks offer bundled packages with preferential rates when you avail both facilities together.
Exceeding your drawing power or sanctioned limit is considered an irregularity and can have several consequences. The bank may charge penal interest (typically 2-3% above the regular rate) on the excess amount. If the overdrawn position persists, the bank may classify the account as irregular, which can affect your credit score and future borrowing capacity. In some cases, the bank may issue a notice to regularize the account within a specified timeframe. To avoid this, monitor your drawing power regularly, submit stock statements on time, and maintain adequate margin in your CC/OD account.
